Progression Mindset

Progression isn't just about trying harder tricks. It's about how you approach learning, assess risk, and know when you're ready for the next step. This is the mental side of getting better.

The Learning Mindset

  • Mistakes are not failures — They are data. Every fall tells you something.
  • Progress is incremental — Small steps add up over time.
  • Comparison is the thief of joy — Focus on your own path.
  • Patience is a skill — It can be trained like anything else.
  • Fear is information — Not a stop sign.
  • Start Small — Build your skills on smaller features before moving to larger ones. The progression ladder exists for a reason.

The Progression Ladder

Every trick or skill exists on a ladder. You can't skip rungs.

  1. Understand the movement — Watch, visualize, talk it through.
  2. Practice on flat ground — Low consequence.
  3. Practice on a smaller feature — Lower consequence.
  4. Practice on the real feature — With full commitment.
  5. Refine and style — Make it clean and controlled.

The Rule

Don't move to the next rung until you're comfortable at the current one.

Finding Your Skill Level

Before you can progress, you need to know where you are.

  • First time on snow or limited experience
  • Riding greens and easy blues
  • Working on falling leaf, J-turns, and linking turns
  • Comfortable on flat base but still learning edge control
  • Comfortable on all blues and easy blacks
  • Linking carved turns consistently
  • Starting to explore park (50-50s, straight airs) or off-piste
  • Can ride switch at a basic level
  • Comfortable on all blacks and double blacks
  • Confident in park (boardslides, 180s, 360s) or steep terrain
  • Rides switch comfortably
  • Can assess terrain and adjust technique accordingly
  • Rides everything on the mountain with control
  • Comfortable with advanced tricks (corks, rodeos, etc.)
  • Can teach others or break down technique
  • Reads terrain instinctively and adapts to conditions

The Rule

Be honest with yourself. Your skill level is where you ride comfortably 90% of the time — not the one run you survived.

Self-Assessment — Knowing When You're Ready

Before you try something new, ask yourself:

  • Have I done this on a smaller feature?
  • Have I seen someone else do it?
  • Can I visualize myself doing it?
  • Am I calm, or am I panicking? (Calm = ready. Panic = not ready.)
  • Do I know what to do if something goes wrong?
  • Have I looked uphill and at the feature before dropping in?

Always Look — Before you drop, look uphill for other riders. Look at the feature for damage or ice. Look at your landing zone to spot it.

The Rule

If you answer "No" to any of these, you're not ready.

Comfort, Stretch, and Panic Zones

  • Comfort Zone — Skills you can do without thinking. You're in control. No risk. This is where you warm up.
  • Stretch Zone — Skills that challenge you. You're slightly uncomfortable but still in control. This is where progression happens.
  • Panic Zone — Skills that overwhelm you. You're not in control. Risk of injury is high.

The Rule

Stay in the Stretch Zone. If you're in the Panic Zone, back up and build more foundation.

Take It Easy — Know your limits. Land on your feet. If you're tired, sore, or distracted, step back. The mountain will still be there tomorrow.

Risk Assessment

01 What's the Worst That Could Happen? +
Be honest with yourself. Broken bone? Concussion? Bruised ego? Knowing the worst-case scenario helps you decide if you're willing to accept the risk.
02 What's the Likelihood of That Happening? +
Based on your current skill level. If you've done this trick on a smaller feature 50 times, the likelihood is low. If you've never tried it, the likelihood is higher.
03 Am I OK With That Risk? +
If yes → commit. If no → step back and build more foundation. There's no shame in waiting.

Safety Nets

01 The Landing Plan +
  • Where will you land?
  • What direction will you be facing?
  • What do you do if you land wrong?
02 The Bailout Plan +
  • How do you get out of this safely?
  • Where is the safe zone?
  • What do you do if it goes wrong?
03 The Spotter +
  • Have someone watch you.
  • They can see things you can't.
  • They can help if you crash.

Mountain Citizenship

Snowboarding is a shared experience. Respect makes it better for everyone.

Respect the Features

  • Features are maintained by park crews
  • Don't ride damaged or closed features
  • Wait your turn — don't cut lines

Respect Other Riders

  • Yield to riders below you
  • Call your drop before entering a feature
  • Don't stop on landings or blind spots
  • Help others when they crash

Respect the Mountain

  • Stay on open terrain
  • Obey closures and signs
  • Pack out what you pack in

The Role of Fear

Fear is not the enemy. It's information.

Fear tells you:

  • You might not be ready (listen to this)
  • You need to slow down (respect this)
  • You care about the outcome (this is good)

What to do with fear:

  • Acknowledge it — Don't ignore it.
