Board Geometry

Your board's geometry describes its shape from different angles. It affects how the board turns, grips the snow, floats in powder, and feels under your feet. The three main areas are Profile, Sidecut, and Shape.

Profile (Vertical Shape)

Profile describes how the board curves along its length when viewed from the side. The main profiles are Camber, Rocker, and Flat, with many boards combining them into hybrid profiles.

01 Camber (Traditional) +

Shape: When placed on a flat surface, the board contacts the ground near the tip and tail while the middle arches upward.

How It Rides:

  • Edge Hold — Provides strong grip on firm and hard snow.
  • Pop — Stores energy when flexed and releases it when the board rebounds.
  • Stability — Feels planted and predictable at higher speeds.
  • Precision — Responds well to controlled and aggressive movements.

Best For: Carving, groomers, hard snow, jumps, and aggressive all-mountain riding.

Drawbacks: Less forgiving than rocker or flat profiles and can feel catchy for beginners. Generally provides less natural float in deep powder.

02 Rocker (Reverse Camber) +

Shape: When placed on a flat surface, the middle of the board contacts the ground while the tip and tail curve upward.

How It Rides:

  • Float — Raised tips help the board stay on top of soft snow.
  • Forgiveness — Reduced edge contact makes catching an edge less likely.
  • Playfulness — Makes presses, butters, and loose turns easier.
  • Maneuverability — Easy to pivot and change direction.

Best For: Powder, park and jibbing, beginners, and playful riding.

Drawbacks: Generally provides less edge hold and stability on hard snow than camber. Can feel less precise at higher speeds.

03 Flat +

Shape: The board is mostly flat along its main riding surface, with little to no camber or rocker.

How It Rides:

  • Predictable — Provides a neutral and consistent feel.
  • Stable — Offers more edge contact than rocker without the tension of camber.
  • Forgiving — Easier to control than a more aggressive camber profile.
  • Versatile — Works well for riders looking for a straightforward ride.

Best For: Beginners, park riding, casual all-mountain riding, and riders who prefer a neutral feel.

Drawbacks: Generally provides less pop and edge response than camber and less float than rocker.

04 Hybrid Profiles +

Most modern snowboards combine camber, rocker, and flat sections to balance different riding characteristics.

Camber-Rocker-Camber
Camber underfoot with rocker toward the tip and tail. Provides edge hold and pop while adding float and easier turn initiation. Best for all-mountain and freeride.
Rocker-Camber-Rocker
Camber through the center with rocker at both ends. Keeps some of the pop and edge hold of camber while feeling looser and more forgiving. Best for all-mountain, powder, and playful riding.
Rocker-Flat-Rocker
A flat center section with rocker at the tip and tail. Provides a stable, predictable feel with added forgiveness and float. Best for beginners, park, and soft snow.
Camber-Flat-Camber
Camber beneath the rider's feet with a flatter section between them. Provides edge hold and pop with a more relaxed feel than full camber. Best for all-mountain and freestyle.
Full Camber
Camber runs continuously from tip to tail. Emphasizes edge hold, pop, and precision. Best for carving, jumps, and aggressive riding.
Full Rocker
Rocker runs continuously from tip to tail. Emphasizes float, maneuverability, and forgiveness. Best for powder, jibbing, and playful riding.

Sidecut (Horizontal Shape)

Sidecut is the inward curve along the edges of the board when viewed from above. It influences how easily the board turns and the shape of those turns.

Sidecut Radius: the radius of the arc created by the board's sidecut. A smaller radius creates tighter turns, while a larger radius creates wider turns.

RadiusFeelBest For
Tight (6–8m)Quick and responsive turnsPark, trees, tight terrain, freestyle
Medium (8–10m)Balanced turn shape and versatilityAll-mountain and most riders
Long (10m+)Wide, sweeping turns with stability at speedCarving, freeride, big mountain, racing
Asymmetrical (Asym)Different sidecut shapes on toe vs. heel edgeRiders wanting a balanced feel between toe- and heel-side turns

How to Choose

Smaller radius = tighter, quicker turns. Larger radius = wider, smoother turns. Asymmetrical = different sidecuts for toe and heel edges.

Board Shapes

Board shape describes how the board is designed from tip to tail. Shape affects how the board rides switch, floats in powder, and handles different terrain.

Twin
The nose and tail are symmetrical or nearly identical. Provides a balanced feel in either direction. Best for park, freestyle, and riding switch.
Directional Twin
Similar to a twin, but with subtle differences between the nose and tail or a slightly setback stance. Combines switch-ability with improved float and stability. Best for all-mountain riding.
Directional
The nose is typically longer or wider than the tail, with the stance positioned farther toward the rear. Helps keep the nose above the snow. Best for powder, freeride, and directional all-mountain riding.
Swallowtail
The tail splits into two points, allowing the tail to sink into deep snow while keeping the nose elevated. Best for powder specialists and deep-snow riding.

Which Geometry Is Right for You?

Riding StyleRecommended Geometry
All-MountainCamber or camber-based hybrid + medium sidecut + directional twin
PowderRocker or rocker-based hybrid + long sidecut + directional or swallowtail
Park / JibbingRocker or flat + tight sidecut + twin shape
Freestyle / JumpsCamber or camber-based hybrid + medium sidecut + twin shape
Carving / RacingCamber + medium-to-long sidecut + directional or twin shape
BeginnerRocker or flat + medium sidecut + twin or directional twin
Aggressive All-MountainCamber or camber-based hybrid + medium-to-long sidecut + directional twin or directional shape

Board geometry works as a combination. Profile affects how the board flexes and contacts the snow, sidecut influences how it turns, and shape determines how it behaves from tip to tail. Together, they define much of a board's overall ride.
