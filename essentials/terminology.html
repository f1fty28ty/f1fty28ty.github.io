Terminology

The complete snowboard glossary — every term a coach needs, organized by category. Start here before anything else on this site will make sense.

01 Stance & Riding Terms +
Regular
Left foot forward. The most common stance.
Goofy
Right foot forward. Named after the Disney character Goofy.
Switch
Riding with the opposite stance from your normal stance.
Forward
Riding in your normal stance, either Regular or Goofy.
Front Foot
The foot closest to the nose of the snowboard.
Back Foot
The foot closest to the tail of the snowboard.
Open Stance
Shoulders and hips face more downhill than the board. Common in freeride and carving.
Closed Stance
Shoulders and hips are more aligned with the board. Common in racing.
Duck Stance
Both feet are pointed outward (e.g., +15° / -15°). Common in freestyle and park riding.
Directional Stance
Also known as Posi-Posi. Both feet are pointed slightly toward the nose (e.g., +18° / +6°). Common in freeride and carving.
02 Edge & Turning Terms +
Toe Edge
The edge of the board on your toe side.
Heel Edge
The edge of the board on your heel side.
Flat Base
Riding with the base of the board flat on the snow, with neither edge significantly engaged.
Edge Engagement
When an edge bites into the snow, providing grip, control, and turning ability.
Carving
Turning with the board's edge strongly engaged in the snow, leaving a clean, narrow line with little or no skidding.
Skidding
Turning while allowing the board to slide partially sideways across the snow, leaving a wider track.
Inclination
Leaning your body toward the inside of a turn to create edge angle.
Angulation
Bending through the knees, hips, and body to create edge angle while keeping the body positioned over the board.
Counter-Rotation
Twisting the upper body opposite to the lower body or board. Used to initiate, control, or quickly change direction.
03 Spin & Rotation Terms +
Frontside (FS)
Jumps: chest opens toward the lip on takeoff. Rails: a normal toeside approach with chest facing the rail.
Backside (BS)
Jumps: chest turns away from the lip on takeoff. Rails: a normal heelside approach with back facing the rail.
Hardway (HW)
The harder edge to perform the trick with, e.g. FS 360 off the toes.
Cork
At takeoff, your torso is further uphill than your feet.
Rodeo
At takeoff, your torso is further downhill than your feet.
Pretzel
A 270°+ rotation in the opposite rotation from which you got on the feature.
Pull Back
Similar to the pretzel but more of a 90° rotation back to your stance prior to getting on the feature.
Sameway (SW)
A 270°+ rotation in the same direction which you spun onto the feature from.
To Fakie
Similar direction as sameway but only rotating the extra 90° to land in your switch stance.
To Forward
Similar to Fakie, but you start switch and come out in your forward stance (either Reg or Goofy).
Shifty
Shifting the board away from the direction of travel and back.
04 Rail & Feature Terms +
50-50
Riding straight over a rail/box with both bindings centered on the feature.
Boardslide
Board perpendicular to the feature.
Lipslide
Approach from one side, jump over the feature, lock into the opposite boardslide position.
Bluntslide
Lock your back binding (or the area just in front) onto the rail.
Tailslide
Lock the tail of the board on the feature.
Noseslide
Lock the nose of the board on the feature.
Press
Leaning heavily onto your front or back foot while sliding, lifting the opposite end.
Nose Press
Pressing on the nose of the board while sliding.
Tail Press
Pressing on the tail of the board while sliding.
Down Rail
A rail that is on a downward pitch.
Down-Flat-Down
A rail with a down section, then a flat section, then another down section. Also known as a double kink.
Kinked Rail
A rail with an angle change in the middle.
Box
A wide, flat feature for sliding. Wider than a rail.
Kicker
A jump in the terrain park.
Tabletop
A jump with a flat top between the lip and the landing.
Step-Up
A jump where the landing is higher than the takeoff.
Step-Down
A jump where the landing is lower than the takeoff.
05 Aerial & Trick Terms +
Straight Air
A jump with no rotation, just air and an optional grab.
Grab
Holding your board in the air.
Nose Grab
Grabbing the nose of the board with your front hand.
Tail Grab
Grabbing the tail of the board with your back hand.
Weddle Grab (Mute)
Grabbing the toe edge between the bindings with your front hand.
Indy Grab
Grabbing the toe edge between the bindings with your back hand.
Melon Grab
Grabbing the heel edge between the bindings with your front hand.
Stalefish Grab
Grabbing the heel edge behind the back binding with your back hand.
Seatbelt Grab
Grabbing the tail with your front hand.
Crail Grab
Grabbing the nose with your back hand.
Wild Cat
A side flip flipping about the tail.
Tame Dog
A side flip flipping about the nose.
Sloth Roll
A slow, controlled forward roll in the air. A style trick.
Double Cork
Two corked rotations in one jump (e.g., Double Cork 1080).
Double Rodeo
Two rodeo rotations in one jump.
06 Terrain & Mountain Terms +
Groomer
A run that has been machine-groomed to create a smooth and consistent snow surface.
Off-Piste
Ungroomed, natural terrain outside of the main groomed trail surface.
Backcountry
Terrain outside a controlled ski-area environment, generally accessed by hiking, snowmobile, helicopter, or other means rather than a chairlift.
Bowl
A large, open area of mountain terrain, often found above or near treeline.
Chute
A narrow, steep section of terrain, often bounded by rocks or other natural features.
Couloir
A narrow, steep mountain gully or channel, often surrounded by rock walls.
Glades
Tree-filled terrain that riders navigate through. Also known as tree runs.
Moguls
Large bumps on a trail formed naturally or through repeated turns by riders and skiers.
Powder
Deep, relatively fresh snow with a soft, low-density texture.
Crud
Churned-up or heavily tracked snow that can become uneven, heavy, or difficult to ride.
Ice
Very hard, dense, or frozen snow that provides little forgiveness and requires precise edge control.
Slush
Wet, soft snow that commonly occurs during warmer conditions, especially in spring.
Corn
Granular snow that has gone through repeated freeze-thaw cycles and softened under the sun. Common during favorable spring riding conditions.
Breakable Crust
A frozen or hardened layer of snow covering softer snow underneath. The crust can break under the rider's weight.
07 Competition & Event Terms +
Slopestyle
A competition with a mix of rails and jumps in a single run.
Halfpipe
A U-shaped course where riders go wall-to-wall and perform tricks in the air.
Big Air
A single, massive jump competition.
Rail Jam
A competition focused solely on rail tricks.
Giant Slalom (GS)
A race with wide, sweeping turns.
Super-G
A faster, more aggressive version of GS.
Parallel Slalom
Head-to-head racing on adjacent courses.
08 Gear & Equipment Terms +
Camber
A board profile with an upward bend in the middle. Provides pop and edge hold.
Rocker
A board profile with a downward bend in the middle. Provides float and forgiveness.
Flat Profile
A board with no camber or rocker. Simple and stable.
Hybrid Profile
A board with a mix of camber and rocker (e.g., rocker-camber-rocker).
Torsional Flex
The board's ability to twist along its length. Determines how easily the board initiates turns and how forgiving it feels. Stiffer torsion = more stability. Softer torsion = easier turn initiation.
Sidecut Radius
The radius of the board's sidecut. Determines turning behavior.
Effective Edge
The length of edge that actually contacts the snow.
Stance Width
The distance between your bindings.
Binding Angles
The angle of your bindings relative to the board.
Highback
The back portion of a binding that supports your heel.
Forward Lean
The forward tilt of the highback.
Strap-In Binding
A traditional binding with two straps (toe and ankle).
Step-On Binding
Burton's system that clicks in without straps.
Overhang
When your toes or heels hang over the edge of the board. Too much = drag.
09 Safety & Etiquette Terms +
Terrain Park Etiquette
The unwritten rules of park riding. Yield to others, call your drop, don't stop on landings.
The Responsibility Code
The official NSAA safety code:
  1. Always stay in control. You must be able to stop or avoid people or objects.
  2. People ahead or downhill of you have the right-of-way. You must avoid them.
  3. Stop only where you are visible from above and do not restrict traffic.
  4. Look uphill and avoid others before starting downhill or entering a trail.
  5. You must prevent runaway equipment.
  6. Read and obey all signs, warnings, and hazard markings.
  7. Keep off closed trails and out of closed areas.
  8. You must know how and be able to load, ride and unload lifts safely. If you need assistance, ask the lift attendant.
  9. Do not use lifts or terrain when impaired by alcohol or drugs.
  10. If you are involved in a collision or incident, share your contact information with each other and a ski area employee.
Park SMART
The NSAA park safety initiative — Start Small, work your way up. Make a Plan, every feature, every time. Always Look, before you drop. Respect the features and other users. Take It Easy — know your limits and land on your feet.
Drop In
Starting your run from the top of a feature.
Call Your Drop
Announcing your entry into the park or onto a feature.
Spotting
Looking at and landing on your intended landing zone.
Avalanche Beacon
A transceiver that signals your location in the event of an avalanche.
Probe
A collapsible pole used to locate buried victims.
Shovel
Used to excavate buried victims.
Avalanche Airbag
A backpack that inflates in an avalanche to keep you on the surface.
