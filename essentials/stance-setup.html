Stance & Setup

Your stance is the most personal part of your snowboard setup. It's where comfort, control, and performance meet. A poorly set-up stance can hold you back for years. A dialed stance makes everything easier.

Why Stance Matters

Your stance is the connection point between your body and your board. It determines:

  • Balance — How stable you feel
  • Power Transfer — How effectively you can pressure the edges
  • Comfort — How long you can ride without pain
  • Style — How your body position looks on the snow
  • Joint Health — Proper alignment prevents knee pain and injury

There is no "perfect" stance for everyone. The best stance is the one that works for you, your riding style, and your body.

Find Your Natural Stance

Before you touch your board, find your natural stance on flat ground:

  1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart.
  2. Squat like you're about to sit in a chair.
  3. Look at your knees.

What you see tells you what you need:

What You SeeWhat It Means
Knees stacked over toes
Perfect. Start here.
Knees tucked inward (knock-kneed)
Stance is too wide. Narrow 1–2 in.
Knees bowing outward
Stance is too narrow. Widen 1–2 in.
Knees tracking past toes
Too much forward lean. Reduce highback lean.

Step 1: Stance Width

Quick Method: Start at shoulder-width. Adjust ±1–2 in based on the squat test above.

HeightStance Width
5'0" – 5'4"18" – 19.5"
5'5" – 5'9"19.5" – 21"
5'10" – 6'2"21" – 22.5"
6'3" +22.5" – 24"

Wider = More stable, better for carving/freeride. Risk: knees tuck in.
Narrower = Easier to spin, better for park. Risk: knees bow out.

Step 2: Binding Angles

Common Stance Types:

TypeAnglesBest For
Duck+15° / -15°Freestyle, park, switch
Moderate Duck+18° / -9°All-mountain
Directional+21° / +6°Freeride, powder, carving
Alpine / Race+30° / +15°Racing

Golden Rule

Back foot angle should never exceed front foot angle.

How Angles Affect Your Knees:

  • Steeper front angle (+21°+) — Knee points forward. Better for carving.
  • Moderate front (+12° to +18°) — Knee points slightly forward. Balanced.
  • Flatter front (+9° or less) — Knee points outward. Easier to spin.
  • Negative back (-15°) — Knee points outward. Easy switch.
  • Positive back (+6°) — Knee points forward. Less switch.

Step 3: Highback Lean

More lean = More responsive, better edge hold, knees push forward.
Less lean = More forgiving, relaxed, better for freestyle.

Riding StyleStarting Point
Freestyle1–2 clicks
All-Mountain2–3 clicks
Carving / Freeride3–4 clicks
RacingMax lean

Signs to adjust: Fatigued legs or a forced-feeling squat → reduce lean. No edge hold → increase lean. Calf pain → reduce lean significantly.

Step 4: Binding Centering

Check: Strap in on flat ground. Look at toe/heel overhang. They should be roughly equal.

  • Too far forward (nose) — Hard to turn. Slide back.
  • Too far back (tail) — Hard to initiate turns. Slide forward.
  • Heel drag or toe drag — Center the binding.

Final Checklist

Before your first run:

  • Squat test: knees track over toes (or slightly outside)
  • Stance width feels balanced
  • Binding angles are comfortable
  • Highbacks rotated to match heel edge
  • Highback lean set to desired feel
  • Bindings centered (no excessive overhang)
  • All hardware tight
